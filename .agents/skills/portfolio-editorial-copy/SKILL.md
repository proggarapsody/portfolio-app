# Portfolio editorial copy

Read the relevant wording evidence and examples in [visual connections](../../../docs/research/VISUAL-CONNECTIONS.md). Read [the confirmed brief](../../../docs/BRIEF.md) for audience/content boundaries and [source synthesis](../../../docs/research/SYNTHESIS.md) for adopted writing methods.

Use the advertising references' hierarchy, not their claims: a short evocative headline, a concrete deck, and precise supporting captions or credits. Write English only unless the owner explicitly changes that decision. Do not reproduce brand slogans or use Japanese words as decoration.

Begin from supplied works, notes and professional evidence. Preserve the author's voice and factual scope. Missing inputs may be marked as editorial placeholders in preparation; never present invented quotes, releases, places, dates, jobs or achievements as publishable facts.

Keep four recognizable section labels: Engineering, Photography, Music, Writing. A poetic issue title or feature heading can accompany them; it must not obscure where employers or readers should go.

For engineering, state the actual problem, the author's role, approach, outcome and available evidence. For photography, use observations, location/date when supplied, and an intentional caption. For music, distinguish artwork, personal notes, release metadata, collaborators and listening destination. For stories, introduce the actual subject with a specific excerpt rather than an inflated promise.

Review a draft for clarity, specificity, voice and unsupported claims. Remove forced symmetry, vague superlatives, invented urgency and keyword repetition. Retain useful details; restraint should not erase dates, credits, project scope or navigation labels.

Return copy in its intended roles with any unresolved factual inputs identified. Attribute quotations and credits. Let SEO use this truthful material for metadata; do not turn the journal into a conversion funnel. Preparing prose does not authorize publication or recurring automation.

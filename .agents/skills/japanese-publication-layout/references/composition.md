## Books and albums: structure rather than costume

The attachments are mainly advertisements and a catalogue cover. They do **not** establish a complete book binding or album package. The following are proposed applications of their hierarchy to the confirmed journal brief, not reconstructions of historical editions.

- **Page frame:** choose a stable text/image area, inner and outer margins, and a consistent line/column rhythm. W3C's Japanese layout guidance calls the underlying layout frame *kihon-hanmen* and relates headings and illustrations to it. For this English site, transfer alignment and proportion; do not mechanically transfer square Japanese character grids, vertical composition or right-to-left reading.
- **Cover → opening:** a strong cover can give way to a quieter title/issue page or contents. Carry a type relationship, rule weight or small color signal across the transition. Do not repeat the cover hero on every page.
- **Facing pages:** let a spread establish a relation—image opposite essay, full scene opposite details, or contents opposite an editorial note. Use the gutter as a pause. Keep faces, key evidence and readable copy clear of a simulated fold. Two columns are not automatically a spread.
- **Album sleeve → liner notes:** an outer image/title creates identity; an inner sequence carries track/work order, personnel, date, credits and an essay. For Music, use actual supplied listening notes and explicit playback. Do not invent tracks, performances or credits to fill a grid.
- **Photo book:** alternate a full image, a paired sequence and a short caption page when the actual series supports that rhythm. The space around an original photo can change; its color and intentional framing remain intact.
- **Running furniture:** a small issue label, section name, folio or index can orient the reader. Give each a real function. Fake prices, archive stamps, nonsense serial numbers and fake Japanese add no editorial content.
- **End matter:** a colophon can disclose real credits, typefaces and production information. It is useful metadata, not a decorative list of fictitious printing details.

See the atlas's opening-sequence diagrams. They are spatial studies, not final page designs. Native URLs/history and direct Engineering access remain usable even when a visual spread metaphor is used. Avoid mandatory page-curl interaction or simulated book controls.

## Image direction before styling

Write a short asset brief: subject, emotional or explanatory role, viewpoint, light/tonal structure, depth or flatness, desired relationship to type, and source/rights. Example: “A detailed studio scene with a diagonal instrument plane and a quiet upper corner for the issue title” is actionable; “retro aesthetic image” is not.

A photograph, original drawing, technical diagram, commissioned illustration and authored typographic plate are distinct choices. Use a suitable available image; if none exists, prepare a labelled art direction or create an appropriate original visual within the task scope. Do not keep reusing the same convenient stock image to satisfy unrelated source families.

Montage requires compatible lighting/scale or deliberate graphic contrast, clean edges, a common compositional field and an editorial idea. Cutouts are not automatically good visuals. Original portfolio photography and supplied music artwork retain their rendering; create separate editorial art instead of silently treating those works.

## Responsive translation and evidence

Decide source order before positioning. Collapse a facing spread into title → image → deck → body/notes; use real headings and captions. Merge newspaper columns into a readable measure, retain selected rules and display contrast, and move side rails into horizontal labels. Keep full-image viewing available for intentionally composed work. Avoid sideways scrolling for required reading.

Compare source and render at thumbnail size and at 100%, at a wide viewport and 390/320px, plus enlarged text. Check the focal point, unintended clipping, line breaks, hierarchy after reflow and whether the visual remains compelling without texture. Functional/accessibility checks are separate evidence. The owner rejected the previous ten variants; passing journeys did not establish their visual quality.

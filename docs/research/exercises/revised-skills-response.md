# Documentation exercise — proposals only

Applied editorial-portfolio-design for reference relationships, issue-art-direction for composition/palette, journal-interaction for navigation states, and portfolio-editorial-copy for factual wording. Read their relevant linked documents; this is specification only.

All three proposals retain the recognizable masthead, navigation, and Engineering / Photography / Music / Writing labels. Publication name, cover selection, issue title, date and section selections remain owner-supplied inputs. Reference scans and brand copy are internal inspiration; they do not become the owner's published work. Original photographs retain their intended colors and rendering: no grain overlays, hue changes, desaturation, blur or interactive scaling.

## A — Ocean signal, reference 2

Use a broad navy feature field around a generously scaled owner-selected photograph. Reference 2 establishes saturated blue surroundings, one dominant visual and subordinate product information; borrow those relationships rather than its wave, watches or branding. Place an English display headline above the image, a short deck in a separate light-on-dark area, and a compact caption immediately below. Strong sans display contrasts with serif reading text and quiet sans metadata. Contents uses four image-led horizontal invitations with varied image widths and clear labels, continuing the navy field without reproducing the cover.

Proposed roles from the atlas: surface #132D5C; reading text #EEF0E2; links/signals #9BBCC8; decorative rules #426CBA. Underline links; do not use decorative blue for untested small text. Preserve the photograph's aspect ratio unless an intentional crop is supplied.

On narrow screens, stack headline, image, deck and caption; wrap headlines at meaningful English phrases. Contents becomes four vertically ordered entries. Keep the image prominent without sideways reading or tiny inset text.

## B — Studio amber, reference 5

Let the selected photograph occupy the upper majority of the cover. Beneath it, use a clearly separate editorial band: a wide expressive serif headline, a concrete deck, then two supporting columns at spacious widths. Reference 5's dark equipment scene over a bright information band provides this image-to-text rhythm. Sans labels identify section, credits and contents positions; body text remains serif.

Roles: dark surround #2B2824; reading text #F4E6CB; link/signal #D58E43; decorative wood-toned rules #7E483C. If the information band uses a light surface, give it a separately tested dark text/link pair rather than carrying cream text onto it. Contents becomes an editorial list with large opening images and smaller supporting captions, rather than another immersive blue spread.

On phones, preserve the full image, place the information band beneath it and collapse supporting columns into reading order. Captions and technical details retain readable sizes. The amber surroundings never become a filter over the artwork.

## C — Newsprint, reference 8

Lead with typography: a strong masthead, oversized English feature headline, one selected portrait or photograph, and ruled supporting bands. Reference 8 supplies dense hierarchical contrast and a dominant title/portrait relationship. Use an asymmetric wide-screen arrangement, with the photograph beside a restrained summary column. A serif display/reading relationship and compact sans indexing distinguish this from both the saturated feature and studio-led cover. Contents uses four indexed, image-led entries with uneven editorial emphasis rather than equal cards.

Roles: paper #F1E2C5; reading ink #28251F; link/title signal #9C352B; decorative rules #C1A47A. Texture belongs to paper furniture only. On narrow screens, headline, photograph, deck and details stack; columns disappear, English stays horizontal, and essential content fits at 320 CSS pixels with enlarged text.

All role colors are proposed web tokens, not recovered inks. The atlas's solid-color ratios do not establish contrast over texture or images; actual rendered type and states need checking later.

## What connects references 1, 3 and 8

Each uses a recognizable large identity/title, a memorable central object or portrait, and much smaller supporting information. Images 1 and 3 give hardware a hero-to-caption hierarchy; 8 achieves theatrical intensity through crowded type and rules. Shared scale contrast does not establish a historical period.

Image 1 appears to print ©1970 SONY; that is a visible source claim. Sony's history linked in VISUAL-CONNECTIONS dates the original Walkman product launch to July 1979, not image 3. Its contemporary-sounding English and unverified provenance remain unresolved. Image 8's supplied 1928 label independently proves neither printing date nor authenticity. The linked Ad Museum history supplies context about advertising changes across eras; no source authenticates all eight scans.
